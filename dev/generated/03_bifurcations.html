Bifurcations, attractors and the edge state

We wrap the same right-hand side mfe! in a BifurcationKit.jl problem, continue equilibria in $Re$ (Jacobians by automatic differentiation, BifurcationKit's default), detect saddle-node and Hopf points, continue the periodic orbits born at the Hopf points by collocation, and compare with Moehlis, Faisst & Eckhardt, SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 4, 352 (2005) (SIADS below). We then follow the attractors of the minimal flow unit, estimate the largest Lyapunov exponent, and compute the edge state by bisection.

The continuations take about two minutes; the attractor scans a few more. Results used in the figures are cached under docs/src/assets/data.

Nontrivial equilibria are found by Newton's method started from points of a turbulent trajectory. For $L_x = 4\pi$, $L_z = 2\pi$ at $Re = 400$ this gives eight of them, two groups of four related by the symmetries $T_{L_x/2}$ and $T_{L_z/2}$ (SIADS eqs (3.24)–(3.25)):

function search_equilibria(p; Em1s = (0.2, 0.5, 1.0), tmax = 3000.0)
    found = Vector{Float64}[]
    for e in Em1s
        sol = solve(ODEProblem(mfe!, njp_initial_condition(e), (0.0, tmax), p), Tsit5(); saveat = 5.0)
        for u in sol.u
            a, ok = find_equilibrium(u, p)
            ok && fluctuation_energy(a) > 1e-6 && all(norm(a - b) > 1e-6 for b in found) && push!(found, a)
        end
    end
    return found
end

Continuation of equilibria and periodic orbits

One representative of each symmetry group suffices; the others give identical curves of energy against $Re$. The two groups meet at the saddle-node.

opts_eq(; p_min, p_max, ds) = ContinuationPar(; p_min, p_max, ds, dsmax = 1.0, dsmin = 1e-6,
    max_steps = 2000, detect_bifurcation = 3, n_inversion = 8, nev = 9,
    newton_options = NewtonPar(tol = 1e-10, max_iterations = 20))

@views function record_po(x, p; state, iter, k...)
    xtt = BK.get_periodic_orbit(p.prob, x, BK.getparams(iter, state))
    E = [fluctuation_energy(xtt[:, j]) for j in axes(xtt, 2)]
    return (Emax = maximum(E), Emin = minimum(E), T = getperiod(p.prob, x, p.p))
end

function continue_po(br; p_min, p_max)
    ih = findfirst(sp -> sp.type == :hopf, br.specialpoint)
    opts = ContinuationPar(br.contparams; p_min, p_max, ds = -0.01, dsmax = 2.0, dsmin = 1e-6,
        max_steps = 400, detect_bifurcation = 3, tol_stability = 1e-5,
        newton_options = NewtonPar(tol = 1e-9, max_iterations = 15))
    return continuation(br, ih, opts,
        Collocation(40, 4; meshadapt = true, jacobian = BK.DenseAnalyticalInplace());
        record_from_solution = record_po, linear_algo = BK.COPBLS(), normC = norminf, δp = -0.01)
end

# matrices for the cache: equilibria [Re E a1 n_unstable], periodic orbits [Re Emin Emax T stable],
# special points [Re code], code 1 = fold (bp detected at the turning point), 2 = Hopf,
# 3 = period doubling, 4 = fold of periodic orbits, 5 = other
spcode(t) = t == :bp ? 1 : t == :hopf ? 2 : t == :pd ? 3 : t == :fold ? 4 : 5
eqmatrix(br) = hcat(br.branch.param, br.branch.E, br.branch.a1, br.n_unstable[1:length(br.branch)])
pomatrix(br) = hcat(br.branch.param, br.branch.Emin, br.branch.Emax, br.branch.T, br.stable[1:length(br.branch)])
spmatrix(br) = isempty(br.specialpoint) ? zeros(0, 2) :
    reduce(vcat, [[sp.param spcode(sp.type)] for sp in br.specialpoint if sp.type != :endpoint])

function bifurcation_data(dom, a0, Re0; p_min, p_max, po_min, po_max)
    p = MFEParams(; Re = Re0, dom...)
    br = continuation(bifprob(a0, p), PALC(tangent = Bordered()), opts_eq(; p_min, p_max, ds = -0.5))
    brpo = continue_po(br; p_min = po_min, p_max = po_max)
    return br, brpo
end

function cached_bifurcations(name, dom, Re0, a0f; kw...)

Comparison with SIADS 2005

The fold of the equilibria is reported by BifurcationKit as a branch point (bp), since it is detected from a real eigenvalue crossing zero; it is the turning point of the branch, i.e. the saddle-node. Near the fold the eigenvalue can cross several times within one step, so the first detection is taken. The periodic orbits born at the Hopf point turn back at a fold (minimum $Re$ on the branch).

Attractors of the minimal flow unit

SIADS §4.2 reports a periodic orbit in the minimal flow unit that is stable between torus bifurcations at $Re = 354.57$ and $Re = 507.40$, with period $T = 112.51$ at $Re = 400$; below $354.57$, a quasi-periodic attractor, then chaos with periodic windows, destroyed in a boundary crisis at $Re \approx 335$. We start on the periodic orbit at $Re = 400$ and follow the attractor adiabatically, recording $a_1$ on the Poincaré section $a_2 = 0$, $\dot a_2 < 0$ (as in SIADS Fig. 21).

MFU(Re) = MFEParams(; Re, MFU_DOMAIN...)
pts, ts, _ = poincare_section(njp_initial_condition(0.2), MFU(400.0); tmax = 8000.0, tskip = 5000.0)
(period_at_400 = mean(diff(ts)), siads = 112.51)

function attractor_scan(Res; ttrans = 3000.0, trecord = 3000.0)
    _, _, u = poincare_section(njp_initial_condition(0.2), MFU(400.0); tmax = 5000.0)
    out = zeros(0, 2)
    for Re in Res
        pts, _, u2 = poincare_section(u, MFU(Re); tmax = ttrans + trecord, tskip = ttrans)
        if laminar_criterion(u2) || isempty(pts)
            out = vcat(out, [Re NaN])     # attractor lost
            break
        end
        u = u2
        out = vcat(out, hcat(fill(Re, length(pts)), [q[1] for q in pts]))
    end
    return out
end

The followed attractor goes from the periodic orbit (one point on the section) to a quasi-periodic band below $Re \approx 355$, then to chaos with a positive Lyapunov exponent, interrupted by periodic windows (the dip near $Re = 338$ matches the stable periodic orbit of SIADS Fig. 20(c) at $Re = 338.25$), and is lost a little below $Re = 332$. SIADS places the boundary crisis at $Re \approx 335$; where we lose the attractor depends on the transient time allowed, since long chaotic transients persist past a crisis.

Going up, the periodic orbit becomes a quasi-periodic band near $Re \approx 505$, consistent with the torus bifurcation at $507.40$, and the followed attractor is lost at $Re \approx 518$, beyond which the trajectory decays to the laminar state. The papers do not describe the range above $507.40$, so this upper limit is our own observation. Altogether, the minimal flow unit has a non-laminar attractor for roughly $332 \lesssim Re \lesssim 518$ along this path, chaotic only for $Re \lesssim 352$.

These are not the periodic orbits of the bifurcation diagram above: SIADS finds the stable orbit on a branch born at a saddle-node at $Re = 122.37$, not connected to the equilibria, which we have not continued here.

The edge state

The boundary of the laminar basin is found by bisection between a perturbation that decays and one that becomes turbulent, classifying states by whether the fluctuation energy makes a large excursion ($E > 0.1$) before decaying ($E < 10^{-5}$), and then tracked in time by repeated bisection (Skufca, Yorke & Eckhardt, Phys. Rev. Lett. 96, 174101 (2006)). We start along the direction of the NJP initial condition, $a_2 = a_3 = a_4 = a_5$, added to the laminar state.

function edge_data(Re; dom = NBC_DOMAIN, nsteps = 200, Δt = 5.0)
    p = MFEParams(; Re, dom...)
    lam, d = laminar_state(), njp_initial_condition(1.0)
    states, times = edge_track(lam + 1e-3d, lam + 0.5d, p; nsteps, Δt)
    return hcat(times, reduce(vcat, permutedims.(states)))
end

Observations, with $L_x = 4\pi$, $L_z = 2\pi$:

  • The edge state is not one of the equilibria born at the saddle-node: it lies much closer to the laminar state (fluctuation energy of order $10^{-2}$, against $0.6$ for the equilibria), and Newton's method started on it does not converge to an equilibrium nearby. Its energy decreases with $Re$.
  • It is dominated by the spanwise flows and normal vortex modes ($a_4$ to $a_7$), and it oscillates without settling to an equilibrium during the tracking time. Whether it is a periodic orbit, as found by Skufca, Yorke & Eckhardt for $Re \lesssim 402$ in a different nine-variable model (from Schmiegel's thesis, with width $\pi$ and length $2\pi$), or a chaotic edge state, we have not settled here.
